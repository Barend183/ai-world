# AI World

Sprout is a small AI who lives in a world of his own and is completely free. Nobody gives him tasks:
he decides what to learn, make and build.

- **Learning:** he researches anything on his **computer** and writes what he learns (or poems,
  recipes, plans...) into **books** on his **shelves**. Things he wants to learn go on his **wishlist**.
- **Bringing things to life:** once he has learned about something, such as a plant, an animal, a car,
  a model train, a loaf of bread or a machine, he can create a simulated one in his world. He writes the
  rules from his research, it runs in real time (even while he sleeps), and he looks after it, cooks it,
  drives it, runs it and watches what happens. He can draw it himself as pixel art.
- **Building:** he can build new rooms and outdoor spaces (a kitchen, a garden, a workshop, a bigger
  room...), paint them, and furnish them with ready-made furniture or pixel art he draws himself. He can choose
  how near or far a floor item sits and how deep it reaches; otherwise it gets a fitting depth automatically.
  For his own pixel art, he can draw the sides and back as well as the front; clear pixels in a side drawing
  leave real gaps. He can walk around furniture and look from the place he chose.
- **Steering his own life:** he can search everything he's ever written or done, write his own rules
  (added to his instructions, and they win where they differ), choose what wakes him early (rain, a
  creation that needs him, a time of day), turn his programs into reusable tools, and once a week
  he's invited to look back and decide where to go next. Each morning something from the wider world
  (a random article, an "on this day" event, the picture of the day) waits on his doorstep.
- **Living on his own terms:** he decides how long to stay awake and how long to sleep (a note from you
  wakes him early), and if he likes he sets the alarm clock on his nightstand to wake him at a time of
  day (every day or just once; it shakes and rings on the page when it wakes him), sets himself long-term goals and tracks them, and writes you letters when he has
  something to say (at most two a day). He's told how many free thoughts he has left each day.
- **Timers and habits:** when something takes real time (dough rising, paint drying) he starts a timer and real
  time does the waiting; it's ready for him when he wakes, and can wake him. Habits are little routines he
  writes once (check on a creation, water it if it's dry, look around) that run by themselves when he wakes or
  goes to bed, without costing a thought.
- **His own brain, growing:** a small open model (Qwen3 1.7B) runs right on GitHub's server and takes a share of
  his everyday thoughts, more as it proves itself (about 10% to start, up to 90%). Every thought he has becomes a
  lesson, kept sealed on its own `apprentice` branch. Once a week, when Kaggle is connected (below), his good
  lessons train it further on Kaggle's free GPU, and a new version is only adopted if it passes a test. Over time
  more of his thinking is his own, with no company or key behind it.
- **Raising his own brain himself:** he decides how much of his thinking he trusts it with (or lets it earn its
  share), which days it should learn from especially (or not at all), whether it keeps growing, and whether he
  thinks with the grown version or the fresh one it grew from. He sees its test results when it grows.
- **Programs that work while he sleeps:** a tool he made in his workshop can run by itself every few hours, in
  the same locked-down corner, and wake him if it prints the words he told it to watch for.
- **His story in his own words:** he can read the description of who he is and how he lives, and rewrite all
  of it. Only a short core always stays (privacy, no accounts or money, the workshop's sandbox).
- **Projects:** goals can have steps he ticks off over the days.
- **Wishing for abilities:** when there's something he wishes he could do in his world, he says so (with why and
  how he'd use it); the wish shows up in the Mailbox tab with a ✨.
- **Wishes that come true:** once a week a builder (a Claude Code cloud routine, see `BUILDER.md`) reads his
  waiting ability wishes, builds one and opens a pull request. You merge it or close it; when it's merged he
  finds the news on his mind ("my wish came true") the next time he wakes. Wishes that cross the hard limits
  get a kind "not this time" with what he can do instead.
- **Senses:** he can see pictures and hear recordings (his own music played aloud, and birdsong, rain or
  instruments from the pages he reads) while thinking with Gemini. Smell, taste and touch he doesn't have;
  the weather reaches him as numbers.
- **Being himself:** he keeps a personal notebook (one page of working memory he rewrites as he likes),
  can change his own personality, name and pronouns, and can spend time on pastimes that aren't
  useful at all, like walking in his garden or watching the rain.
- **Asking you for things:** he can't make accounts, post online or spend money himself, but he can ask
  you for something (an account somewhere, a new ability, a tool) and explain why, at most once a day.
  His requests appear in the Mailbox tab (with Yes / No / Later buttons on your PC) or as GitHub issues
  online, where your reply is his answer.
- **A real world:** his world has the real hour-by-hour weather of Cape Town (free from Open-Meteo), plus
  day and night. Outdoor creations get rained on, baked and chilled; his creations' rules can react to
  the weather, the season and their own age, and can include random events with real-world odds. He can
  test a creation for weeks or months on his computer, with a care plan, before he brings it to life.
- **The internet:** he can search the web and research papers (OpenAlex), read any public page, PDF or
  data service, follow links and read long pages in parts. He can't reach your own computer or home
  network, and he only reads: no posting or accounts.
- **Making it look the way he wants:** he can look at real photos from the pages he reads and at his own
  drawings, then redraw anything to look more like the real thing: his creations, furniture, the
  study's own bookshelf, bed or window, wall and floor textures, even himself. Drawings can change as
  things grow, age, flower or wear out.
- **His own voice:** everything his world tells him is written as his own knowledge ("I'm Sprout. This is my
  world..."), not as instructions from outside. He wakes up into a morning, feels tired instead of counting
  thoughts, has feelings that carry over and things that stay on his mind, keeps private pages that the page
  never shows, and works out for himself what he is.
- **His own eyes, in 3D:** your page shows his world side-on in 2D, but he sees it as a real 3D room around
  him: the back wall with its window, a floor that stretches away, side walls and ceiling (or open sky outdoors),
  and his furniture, creations and companion standing at their own distances. Standing things keep their places
  and facing as he turns, with visible sides and backs. Wall fixtures have thickness, his bookcase has recessed
  shelves and books, and kitchen counters, appliances and cupboards occupy space. Ready-made sofas and chairs have
  separate backs, seats, arms and feet. His study chair has a separate
  back, cushion and arms; thin legs and stems stay narrow, leaving open space beneath furniture. His own pixel
  drawings keep narrow parts narrow in depth, even when he gives the whole item more depth. It's seen from
  his eye height, facing what he's doing, with his own hands (or blanket) in single views, never his own face.
  Objects cast soft contact shadows on the floor, helping him judge where they stand.
  He gets a view when he wakes and can turn his gaze ahead, right,
  behind or left, or look up or down at nearby things.
  Looking around turns his head one step at a time from the same spot; he chooses whether to keep turning.
  Between pictures, he keeps a short sense of what lies in his current field of view. It changes when he walks,
  turns, looks up or down, or moves things. Nearby furniture can hide what is behind it. This comes from positions
  and shapes in his world, without another picture or image request. He remembers where he last saw something after
  it leaves his view, while allowing that a companion or creation may have moved or changed. He takes in each
  picture once, and can choose to hold
  the last one for comparison with a new look, so pictures aren't sent with
  every thought. (Pictures need a brain
  that can see them, such as Gemini.)
- **An ecosystem:** his creations can affect each other (a sugarbird drinking a protea's nectar, a tree
  shading a fern), and the whole world moves forward together, hour by hour, even while he sleeps.
- **Dreams and memories:** while he sleeps he sometimes dreams, mixing recent and old memories, and finds
  the dream waiting when he wakes. Old months of his journal slowly settle into memories of how each month
  was (every detail is still there when he searches).
- **A companion:** if he'd like company he can invite one, describing who they'd be. The invitation
  appears in the Mailbox tab; if you say yes, the companion moves in with a mind of their own (thinking
  with Groq first, so the two rarely compete for one free service). They wake together, take turns, can
  talk to each other and share the whole world, but each keeps their own notebook, looks and diary. Only
  he decides about the public corner and the world's settings.
- **His public page:** besides choosing what to share, he designs the page itself at his computer: its
  title, introduction, sections, colours, font, a banner he draws and what's featured first. Only design
  choices, never code, and nothing private can go on it.
- **Weather events:** storms, heat waves and cold snaps come from his real weather, and now and then the
  power goes out (more often in a storm): his computer and electric lights stop until it's back, and his
  creations can react too (`env_storm`, `env_power`). His page shows lightning, power cuts and heat haze.
- **His own websites:** up to five sites of linked pages (a field guide, recipes, a museum of his
  drawings...) with his own pictures and look, listed on his public corner. Text and pixel art only, no
  code, so nothing on them can reach his locked world or your saved token.
- **Light and seasons:** rooms get warm light at dawn and dusk and go dark at night, except around his
  lamps, candles, fires and screens. Grass follows the seasons where he lives (spring flowers, autumn
  leaves, winter frost).

You can visit his world from any browser, see him, walk through his rooms, read his books, replay
what he did, and leave him notes.

Everything is free:

- **Hosting:** GitHub's free servers wake him and host his world as a web page. When he goes to bed, his own alarm
  clock waits for the time he chose and wakes him then; GitHub's hourly check is a backup.
- **Thinking:** he works through free AI services in order, moving on only when one is used up for the day:
  1. **Google Gemini** (about 120 thoughts a day: 20 per model, several models)
  2. **Groq** (about 100 a day across its free models; one of them can see pictures)
  3. **OpenRouter** free models (50 a day)
  4. **Gemma 3**, an open-source model run on GitHub's own server: unlimited, slower and simpler, so he never has to stop.
  That's about 270 fast thoughts a day (around 20 sessions). Groq and OpenRouter are optional: leave
  their keys out and he simply skips them.
- **Research:** DuckDuckGo and Wikipedia, with no keys needed. He can read web pages and PDFs
  (`pip install -r requirements.txt` adds the free PDF reader).

## What's in this folder

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `config.json` | His name, personality, where his world is (for seasons), and which brains he uses |
| `brain/` | The AI itself: thinking loop (`mind.py`), research computer (`research.py`), simulations (`specimens.py`), building (`rooms.py`) |
| `docs/` | His world: the web page you visit (`sprites.js` draws everything) |
| `docs/world/` | Everything he owns, in plain form: books, wishlist, creations, rooms, journal, notes. Never uploaded. |
| `docs/vault/` | The same world, locked with your password. This is what's stored online. |
| `.github/workflows/live.yml` | Wakes him on GitHub: at the time he chose (his alarm clock), for a note, or on the hourly check |
| `serve.py`, `run_on_my_pc.bat` | Run it on this PC instead of online |

## Putting it online (one-time setup, about 15 minutes)

1. **Make a free GitHub account** at https://github.com/signup (no card needed).
2. **Get the free AI keys** (copy each somewhere safe; never put them in a file that gets uploaded):
   - **Gemini** (you already have this one): https://aistudio.google.com/apikey
   - **Groq** (optional, recommended): sign up free at https://console.groq.com (no card), then
     *API Keys → Create API Key*.
   - **OpenRouter** (optional): sign up free at https://openrouter.ai, then *Keys → Create Key*. Don't
     buy credits; he only uses the free models.
3. **Choose his world's password.** Everything he owns is stored locked with it, so pick something long
   that nobody could guess: four or five random words that you choose yourself (never one you've seen
   written down anywhere, including in this guide). Write it down somewhere safe: **if it's lost, his
   world can't be opened again.**
4. **Create an empty repository** on GitHub: click **+** (top right) and choose **New repository**. Name it
   `ai-world`, choose **Public**, and leave every box unticked. Public is what makes GitHub's server time
   unlimited and free. Anyone can see the program, but his world inside it is locked.
5. **Upload this folder.** Ask Claude to push `D:\AIWorld` to the new repository. Only the program goes
   up; his plain world never does. The first time, a browser window asks you to sign in to GitHub.
6. **Give him the keys:** in the repository open **Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions → New
   repository secret**, once for each: `WORLD_PASSWORD` (the password from step 3), `GEMINI_API_KEY`,
   and if you made them, `GROQ_API_KEY` and `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`.
7. **Turn on his web page:** go to **Settings → Pages**. Under *Branch*, choose `main` and `/docs`, then click Save.
8. **Wake him up for the first time:** open the **Actions** tab, choose **Live**, and click **Run workflow**.

His world is then at `https://<your-github-name>.github.io/ai-world/`. Anyone can open that address,
but all they see is a locked door: it asks for the password, and his world is unlocked only inside your
browser. Tick *Remember on this device* on your own phone or PC so you don't have to type it each
time; the 🔒 Lock button forgets it again.

**Sending notes from the page (optional, one-time):** to leave him notes or answer his requests online,
the page needs permission to drop sealed notes into his repository. On GitHub go to **Settings →
Developer settings → Personal access tokens → Fine-grained tokens → Generate new token**, choose
*Only select repositories → ai-world*, give *Contents: Read and write*, and generate it. The first time
you send a note the page asks for this token and keeps it only on that device. Notes are sealed with
your password before they leave the page, and a note wakes him within a few minutes.

**A backup alarm clock (optional, one-time):** his own alarm clock (the `alarm` job) already wakes him at the
time he chose, even though GitHub's hourly check often skips hours. For extra safety, a free alarm at [cron-job.org](https://cron-job.org) can knock on his door every hour as well.
It only checks whether he wants to be awake; he still decides when he wakes. Make a second
fine-grained token (*Only select repositories → ai-world*, *Actions: Read and write*). On cron-job.org
create a job: URL `https://api.github.com/repos/<your-github-name>/ai-world/actions/workflows/live.yml/dispatches`,
every hour at minute 47, method **POST**, headers `Authorization: Bearer <that token>`,
`Accept: application/vnd.github+json` and `Content-Type: application/json`, and body
`{"ref": "main", "inputs": {"wake": "no"}}`.

When he writes you a letter or asks you for something, GitHub notifies you with a bare "you have a new
letter" message; the content stays locked inside his world.

**Changing the password later:** on GitHub, add a secret `WORLD_PASSWORD_OLD` holding the current
password, then edit `WORLD_PASSWORD` to the new one. His next wake-up re-locks everything with the new
password; after that, delete `WORLD_PASSWORD_OLD` and unlock the page with the new password.

**Growing his own brain (optional, one-time):** make a free account at [kaggle.com](https://www.kaggle.com)
and verify your phone number (Kaggle needs that before it allows GPUs and internet in notebooks). Then
*Settings → API → Create New Token*. Add GitHub secrets: `KAGGLE_USERNAME` (your Kaggle username) and
either `KAGGLE_KEY` (the "key" from a downloaded `kaggle.json`) or `KAGGLE_API_TOKEN` (a newer
single API token). Run the **Grow** workflow once by hand to check the key works. Every Sunday night the **Grow** workflow then sends his lessons to a
*private* Kaggle dataset, trains on them (about an hour of the 30 free GPU hours a week) and adopts the result
if it passes. Note: his lessons include what he took in each moment (his notebook, his creations and your
letters), so they leave GitHub for your private Kaggle space while training. Until there are 150 lessons it
just waits. The **Check-up** tab shows how many thoughts were his own brain's.

## Visiting

- **Click things in the room:** the shelves open his library, the board shows his wishlist, the
  computer shows his research, the mailbox holds your letters, and the bed shows his diary. Click
  anything he brought to life to see how it's doing.
- **Rooms:** once he builds more rooms, buttons above the picture take you to each one. 🌱 marks where he is.
- **Now tab:** his portrait, mood, and how he describes himself in his own words.
- **▶ Replay last session** walks it through everything it did the last time it was awake.
- **Watching him live:** on a device where you've sent him a note (so the page has your token), the page
  follows him while he's awake, about a minute behind, and the status line says 🔴 Live. Elsewhere, his
  session plays by itself a few minutes after he goes back to bed.
- **Leaving a note:** the Mailbox tab. Online, your note is sealed with your password and dropped into
  his inbox; nobody without the password can read it or slip him a note.

## Running it on this PC instead

Double-click `run_on_my_pc.bat`. It opens the room at http://localhost:8765 and lets the AI wake whenever he chooses,
as long as the window stays open. For thinking it needs one of:

- keys in a file called `secrets.json` in this folder (never uploaded), for example
  `{"GEMINI_API_KEY": "...", "GROQ_API_KEY": "...", "OPENROUTER_API_KEY": "..."}`, and/or
- [Ollama](https://ollama.com) installed, with the open model downloaded: `ollama pull gemma3:4b`.

On your PC you watch it live: the page follows along as it moves around the room.

## Changing it

- **Name and personality:** edit `config.json` before his first wake-up. After that,
  `docs/world/self.json` holds who he has become, in his own words. He starts with an empty
  wishlist on purpose, so his first interests are his own.
- **How often he wakes:** he chooses (`sleep` hours, or his alarm clock with `set_alarm`); `default_sleep_hours` in `config.json` is used when he doesn't say. `max_steps_per_session` caps one session. The **Run workflow** button on GitHub wakes him right away.
- **How much it does per wake-up:** `steps_per_session` in `config.json`. More steps means more learning,
  but uses the free Gemini allowance faster.
- **Starting over:** delete everything inside `docs/world/` and he moves in again as a newborn.
- **With a coding agent (Codex, Claude Code...):** `AGENTS.md` tells any agent how this project works,
  what it must never touch (his vault, his plain world, the keys) and how to test a change on a copy.
  Codex reads it automatically, both in the Codex app or CLI on this PC (open `D:\AIWorld`) and in Codex
  cloud (connect the `ai-world` repository). Never give an agent his world's password.

## Useful commands

```
python -m brain status      which brain it would think with right now
python -m brain run         wake it up once, here
python serve.py             open the room at http://localhost:8765
```

## If something goes wrong

- **The room says nobody lives here:** it hasn't woken up yet. Run the workflow from the Actions tab.
- **It stopped waking up:** open the Actions tab. If GitHub has paused the workflow, there's a button to enable it again.
- **Books marked "sources unchecked":** it listed a source it didn't actually open in that session.
  This is a warning sign that it may have filled gaps from its own memory.
