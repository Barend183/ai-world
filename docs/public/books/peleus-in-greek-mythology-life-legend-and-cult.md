# Peleus in Greek Mythology: Life, Legend, and Cult

## Introduction
Peleus (Ancient Greek: Πηλεύς) is a central figure in Greek heroic mythology. King of Phthia in Thessaly, husband of the Nereid Thetis, and father of the Trojan War hero Achilles, Peleus embodies the archaic heroic archetype—a mortal champion who interacted intimately with gods, centaurs, and monsters.

## Origins and Early Exiles
Peleus was born to Aeacus, King of Aegina, and Endeïs, an oread of Mount Pelion. Alongside his brother Telamon (father of Ajax), Peleus joined Jason as an Argonaut in search of the Golden Fleece.

His early life was marred by tragedy and violence:
- **The Slaying of Phocus:** Peleus and Telamon killed their half-brother Phocus, leading to their banishment from Aegina by King Aeacus.
- **Exile in Phthia:** Seeking refuge, Peleus was purified by King Eurytion of Phthia and married Eurytion's daughter Antigone. However, during the famous Calydonian Boar Hunt, Peleus accidentally killed Eurytion with his spear and had to flee Phthia.
- **Exile in Iolcus and Mount Pelion:** In Iolcus, King Acastus purified Peleus. Astydamia, Acastus's wife, attempted to seduce Peleus. When spurned, she falsely accused him of rape. Acastus took Peleus hunting on Mount Pelion, hid his sword in cow dung, and abandoned him to be killed by wild centaurs. Peleus was saved when either Hermes, Hephaestus, or the wise centaur Chiron restored his weapon. Peleus later conquered Iolcus and killed Astydamia.

## Marriage to Thetis and the Apple of Discord
Because of a prophecy that the sea-nymph Thetis would bear a son greater than his father, Zeus arranged her marriage to a mortal to prevent a threat to divine order. Peleus ambushed Thetis on the shores of Cape Sepias, holding fast while she transformed into fire, water, and wild beasts, until she submitted.

Their wedding feast at Mount Pelion was attended by the Olympian gods, who brought divine gifts:
- **Poseidon:** The immortal horses Balius and Xanthus.
- **Hephaestus:** A golden knife.
- **Aphrodite:** A embossed bowl.
- **Athena:** A flute.
- **Zeus & Hera:** Divine chlamys and wings of Arke.

During this banquet, Eris (goddess of discord), enraged at being uninvited, cast the golden **Apple of Discord** marked "To the Fairest," sparking the competition between Hera, Athena, and Aphrodite that led to the Judgment of Paris and the Trojan War.

## Fatherhood and Achilles
Thetis bore seven sons to Peleus, six of whom perished in her attempts to make them immortal. The youngest, Achilles, survived:
- In popular tradition, Thetis dipped Achilles into the River Styx, holding him by his heel.
- In earlier tradition, Peleus caught Thetis burning away Achilles' mortal flesh over fire and anointing him with ambrosia; interrupted, she abandoned father and son in anger.

Peleus entrusted the young Achilles to the centaur Chiron on Mount Pelion to be raised in medicine, music, and martial arts. In his old age, Peleus gave Achilles his invincible ash spear and divine horses for the campaign against Troy.

## Cult and Legacy
While Peleus lacked a central temple complex in Aegina, historical inscriptions and fragments (such as Callimachus's *Aitia*) confirm a hero-cult dedicated to Peleus on the Aegean island of Ikos (modern Alonissos), where he was venerated as king of the Myrmidons. In classical Greek drama, Peleus appears as a tragic, dignified elder in Euripides's *Andromache* and Sophocles' lost play *Peleus*.
