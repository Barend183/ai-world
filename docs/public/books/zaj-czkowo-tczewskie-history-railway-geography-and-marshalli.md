# Zajączkowo Tczewskie: History, Railway Geography, and Marshalling Infrastructure of Pomerania

## Introduction
Zajączkowo is a village located in the administrative district of Gmina Tczew, within Tczew County, Pomeranian Voivodeship, in northern Poland. Situated within the ethnocultural region of Kociewie in historical Royal Prussia/Pomerania, Zajączkowo is historically renowned across Polish railway history for hosting **Zajączkowo Tczewskie**, one of the largest and most strategically significant freight marshalling yards (*stacja rozrządowa*) in Central Europe.

Positioned immediately north of the major junction city of Tczew, Zajączkowo Tczewskie was developed to bypass passenger traffic at Tczew station and handle heavy freight corridors connecting Upper Silesian coalfields and southern agricultural belts directly to the Baltic Sea ports of Danzig (Gdańsk) and Gdynia.

---

## Historical Development and Construction (1910–1917)

### Prussian Origins
Construction of the freight yard at Zajączkowo began in 1910 under the Royal Prussian State Railways (*Königlich Preußische Staatseisenbahnen*). As freight traffic across the Vistula River bridges at Tczew (Dirschau) intensified, the existing station layout was unable to cope with shunting requirements. Engineers designed a direct track—colloquially termed the *królewiecki* (Königsberg) track—allowing freight trains arriving over the Vistula bridge to bypass Tczew passenger station and proceed straight into Zajączkowo's arrival tracks.

### Early Junction Links
Between 1912 and 1913, key interconnecting lines were constructed:
- **Suchostrzygi station** and the **Malinowo junction** linked Zajączkowo directly to the Chojnice line (*Linia kolejowa 203*), facilitating east-west transit.
- The onset of World War I in 1914 dramatically accelerated construction due to military transport needs.
- Full operational capability was achieved in **1917**, marked by the commissioning of mechanical signal towers, interlocking equipment, and the single-track connector line (*łącznica*) between Górki and Zajączkowo.

---

## Interwar Era and the Polish Coal Trunk-Line (1920–1939)

Following the Treaty of Versailles, Pomerania returned to Poland as part of the Second Polish Republic (*Pomeranian Voivodeship*). Zajączkowo Tczewskie became a key node of the Polish State Railways (PKP).

### The Port of Gdynia and Coal Corridors
With the Free City of Danzig exercising customs disputes against Poland, the Polish state built the new deep-water port at Gdynia and constructed the **Polish Coal Trunk-Line** (*Magistrala Węglowa*). Zajączkowo served as the principal northern marshalling and sorting yard where heavy freight trains carrying Upper Silesian coal were reassembled before final movement to Gdynia or Gdańsk.

In 1928, major tracks and switching apparatus in the ZTB signal district were overhauled to withstand growing axle loads and heavy traffic volumes.

---

## Technical Layout and Signal Districts

Zajączkowo Tczewskie was engineered as a multi-stage gravity and hump yard consisting of three main operational signal districts (*okręgi nastawcze*):

1. **ZTA District**: Arrival and sorting group for northbound traffic.
2. **ZTB District**: Central hump and sorting yard with extensive underground cable networks, drain systems, and mechanical switching control.
3. **ZTC District**: Departure and storage groups, expanded significantly in 1964 with relay interlocking signaling systems (*przekaźnikowy system srk*).

### Maintenance Infrastructure
To support heavy motive power, Zajączkowo contained a First-Class Main Engine Shed (*Parowozownia Główna I Klasy*) and comprehensive wagon repair shop (*wagonownia*). A dedicated worker shuttle train, locally known as the ***pendel***, operated between Tczew passenger station and Zajączkowo to transport railway personnel across the expansive facility.

---

## Motive Power: The Prussian Tp4 / G 8.1

For decades, heavy shunting and local freight transit at Zajączkowo were dominated by the **Tp4** steam locomotive (Prussian Class **G 8.1**). Over 5,000 units of this 0-8-0 (D-n2t/D-h2) freight engine were built between 1912 and 1921. Known for high tractive effort on low-speed freight movements, the Tp4 performed heavy hump switching at Zajączkowo long after mainline routes electrified, with some units remaining active in industrial service into the 1980s.

---

## Modern Status and Legacy

During the peak industrial era of the 1960s and 1970s, Zajączkowo handled thousands of freight cars daily. Major modernizations occurred between 1983 and 1988, expanding the wagon repair hall and locomotive depot facilities.

Until December 2009, Zajączkowo Tczewskie held classification as one of Poland's top 10 primary freight marshalling yards (*stacja rozrządowa*). Following restructuring in rail freight dynamics and unit-train operations, its official status transitioned to a shunting/switching station (*stacja manewrowa*), while continuing active vehicle repair and maintenance operations under **PKP Cargo Tabor Pomorski**.

Zajączkowo remains an enduring architectural and technical symbol of Pomerania's rich industrial heritage and strategic role in Baltic transit geography.
