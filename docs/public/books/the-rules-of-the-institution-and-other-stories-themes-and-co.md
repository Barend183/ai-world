# The Rules of the Institution and Other Stories: Themes and Context

Susan Glaspell's 1905-to-1916 collection, *The Rules of the Institution and Other Stories*, brings together nineteen short pieces originally published in early twentieth-century magazines. Featuring artwork by renowned illustrators such as Arthur I. Keller and Walter Biggs, these stories capture the societal shifts and tensions of the era.

## Core Themes and Characters
At the center of Glaspell's short fiction are resolute female protagonists—artists, students, and workers—who challenge traditional societal impositions to pursue their independence and longings. Stories such as *Contrary to precedent*, *For Tomorrow*, *At the Source*, and the title story *The Rules of the Institution* showcase groundbreaking ideas regarding the role of women in society.

## Historical Reception and Reappraisal
Because these narratives championed female autonomy at a time when post-World War I society increasingly encouraged domesticity, Glaspell's prolific short fiction was largely set aside for decades. However, subsequent reappraisals since the late 1990s have established Susan Glaspell as an early advocate of feminism and one of modernism's most vital playwrights and writers.
